## Interpretation

- The fixed topology's ready-path service ceiling is approximately 312 tasks/s.
- The highest sustainable tested point is 300 RPS. It is close to the capacity
  knee and already shows 88 ms p95 queue latency.
- A practical operating target with headroom is approximately 250 RPS.
- The worker path is the bottleneck. Enqueue remained below 1 ms p95 even at
  800 RPS, while worker throughput stopped increasing.
- Two serial worker processes provide roughly 155 tasks/s each for this
  execution-time distribution and acknowledgement behavior.

## Suggestions

1. Add bounded in-process worker concurrency before optimizing producers or the
   retry mover.
2. Re-run this exact sweep after concurrency lands and compare directly with
   this run.
3. Keep 200 and 300 RPS as regression points, 325 RPS as a knee detector, and
   400 RPS as an overload test.
4. For a 50,000 RPS production target, avoid scaling the current serial design
   to hundreds of worker processes; use bounded handler concurrency and then
   measure the next bottleneck.

## Caveats

- Runs used a local arm64 laptop, one Dockerized Kafka broker, one ready
  partition, two producers, two serial workers, and one retry mover.
- Each arrival point ran once. Repeat selected points before using small
  differences as authoritative capacity changes.
- The workload simulated 5 ms average, 10 ms p95, and 20 ms p99 handler time;
  different execution distributions or payload sizes will move the capacity
  knee.
- Absolute local numbers are diagnostic. The shape of the plateau and latency
  growth is the stronger result.
